**Socratic Question:** If an options market maker didn't exist, why would the market fail? How does your simulation's "Man vs. Nature" setup mirror the real-world tension of providing liquidity under uncertainty?
* **Instruction:** Generate a title slide. Motivate the agent as a "Walrasian Auctioneer" who has been fired and replaced by a Reinforcement Learning algorithm.

## Slide 2: From ODEs to Algorithms
**Socratic Question:** In class, we solve for Nash Equilibrium using fixed-point theorems. In your sim, the equilibrium isn't *given*; it *emerges*. How do you define "equilibrium" when the agent is constantly learning?
* **Instruction:** Create a slide titled **"Constructive vs. Analytical Rationality"**. Contrast the "God's eye view" of classical game theory with the "Agent's eye view" of your simulation.

---

## Slide 3: The Game Against Nature
**Socratic Question:** Is Nature a strategic opponent or just a stochastic process? If Nature "plays" a Geometric Brownian Motion, what is the agent's **Best Response Function**? 
* **Instruction:** Use a diagram tag here: ``. Explicitly link your `pick_a_delta()` function to the concept of a **Statistical Decision Rule** (Wald, 1950).

---

## Slide 4: The Agent's Mind (The Mental Sim)
**Socratic Question:** Why does the agent need a "mental sim" instead of just looking at a formula? How does this represent **Bounded Rationality** (Simon) or **Subjective Expected Utility** (Savage)?
* **Instruction:** Explain the **Thompson Sampling** logic. Use LaTeX to show the transition from the Beta Prior to the Posterior belief about market direction.
* **Formula Hint:** $\alpha_{t+1} = \alpha_t + \mathbb{1}_{\{S_{t+1} > S_t\}}$

---

## Slide 5: The "Black-Scholes" Tension
**Socratic Question:** Your agent is "bad" at being a Black-Scholes hedger. Is that a bug in the code, or a feature of reality? Is a "Risk-Neutral" hedge even possible for an agent with finite computing power and a discrete choice set?
* **Instruction:** Present the "Mismatch" as a discovery. Contrast **Dynamic Hedging** (the theory) with **Static/Bandit Hedging** (your simulation).

---

## Slide 6: Results & The "So What?"
**Socratic Question:** Looking at your `delta_comparison.png`, what does the "Average distance from True BS Delta" actually tell us about the cost of learning? 
* **Instruction:** Create a slide for "Emergent Behaviors." Discuss whether the agent is **Speculating** (chasing drift) or **Hedging** (minimizing variance).

---

## Slide 7: Future Directions (Nested Simulations)
**Socratic Question:** If you gave the agent the ability to "re-hedge" inside its own mind (nested loops), would it finally discover the Black-Scholes identity? Or is the search space too large for a lone bandit?
* **Instruction:** Final slide on "The Horizon." Connect back to **Agent-based Computational Economics (ACE)**.
